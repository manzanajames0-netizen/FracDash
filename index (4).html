What is a Fraction?

A fraction represents a part of a whole. In scientific (standard mathematical) form it is written as a vertical ratio of two integers:

Here a is the numerator — how many parts you have — and b is the denominator — how many equal parts the whole was split into (b can never be zero).

Why Fractions Matter

  • Everyday measuring: recipes (¾ cup sugar), tools (½ inch bolt), and portions all rely on fractions.
  • Money & time: "a quarter" of a peso, half an hour, ⅓ of a work shift.
  • Science & construction: precise measurements, scale models, and blueprints use fractional units.
  • Foundation for algebra: ratios, rates, probability, and equations all build on fraction skills.
  • Fair sharing: splitting a mango pie evenly between friends is a fraction problem in disguise!

🔍 The 12 Types of Fractions

Each card below shows the formula in scientific (stacked) notation, a visual model, and the step-by-step process for identifying or working with that type.

Bonus Process: Adding Dissimilar (Unlike) Fractions

1. Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD) of both fractions.
2. Convert each fraction into an equivalent fraction using the LCD.
3. Add the numerators and keep the common denominator.
4. Simplify the result if it's reducible. Example: 1/3 + 1/4 → LCD 12 → 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

🥭 Real-Life Practice Questions

Worked examples using everyday, situational problems — the same style you'll see in Fraction Match and Mango Falling.

Q1. Wilson ate 3/8 of a mango pie, then later ate another 2/8. How much of the pie did he eat in total?

Same denominator → these are like fractions, so add the numerators directly: 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8.

Answer: 5/8 of the pie.

Q2. A vendor had 3/4 kg of mangoes and sold 1/3 kg to Kuya Ben. How many kilograms are left?

These are unlike fractions (denominators 4 and 3) → LCD is 12 → 9/12 − 4/12 = 5/12.

Answer: 5/12 kg left.

Q3. The class collected ₱45 out of a ₱100 goal for a mango-tree planting project. What decimal portion of the goal is that?

45/100 has a denominator that's a power of 10 → shift the decimal point 2 places: 45/100 = 0.45.

Answer: 0.45 (45% of the goal).

Q4. A tailor needs a ribbon that measures 11/4 meters. Write this length as a mixed number.

Divide: 11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3 → whole number 2, remainder becomes the new numerator over the same denominator.

Answer: 2 ¾ meters.

Q5. Rusty collected 2/5 of a basket of mangoes and Nena collected 3/5. Whose share is bigger, and by how much?

Same denominator, so just compare numerators: 3 > 2 → Nena's share is bigger. Difference: 3/5 − 2/5 = 1/5.

Answer: Nena's share is bigger by 1/5.

Q6. A garden plot is divided into 12 equal rows; 8 rows are planted with mangoes. Simplify the fraction of rows planted.

GCF of 8 and 12 is 4 → divide both numerator and denominator by 4: 8/12 → 2/3.

Answer: 2/3 of the rows (a reducible fraction, now in lowest terms).
